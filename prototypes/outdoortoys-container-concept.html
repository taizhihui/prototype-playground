Container Concept · OUTDOOR TOYS

One anchor purchase.
A 30-day window. Then silence.

Outdoor Toys sells the centerpiece of a family's garden: climbing frames, swing sets, trampolines and playhouses, most of them £250 and up. It is a high-consideration purchase made by a parent, qualified on the child's age first and the garden second, and for most families it happens once. Only 4.7% of first-time buyers ever place a second order, and 41% of the shopper base is dormant.

But the buying behaviour has a clear rhythm. When a second purchase does happen, it happens fast: the median gap is 15 days, 70% land within 30 days of the first order, and the basket is almost always an add-on to the anchor they just bought, like swing anchor kits, baby swing seats, trapeze bars and ride-on chargers. After day 30, the moment is gone. The longer arc of the relationship follows the child: kids age up, siblings arrive, and the garden grows in stages around occasions like spring and Christmas.

That shape of demand is what the container model is built for: a short, high-value attach window after every anchor purchase, and a long, slow re-activation cycle driven by the family rather than the product. Here is what that looks like for Outdoor Toys.

Container Intelligence
Behavioral inputs that drive this container's decisions
Attach Window
0–30d
70% of second orders land by day 30
When Outdoor Toys shoppers buy again, they do it fast — median 15 days from first order, 70% within 30 days. After day 30 the attach moment is gone. Container concentrates all outreach inside this window, after delivery confirms.
Attach Path
Frame → Anchors & Seats
What second orders actually contain
Top second-order items: swing anchor kits, baby and toddler swing seats, trapeze bars, rope ladders, ride-on chargers. Container matches the add-on to the exact frame they own and the ages of their kids.
Suppression Logic
5 conditions
When the container holds
Delivery still in transit · Active conversation open · Support or assembly ticket unresolved · Message sent within 7 days · Shopper replied "not now" within 48h. Container holds and re-evaluates on the next signal — does not skip.
Signal Hierarchy
SA › Cart › Browse
Intent ranking
Container leads with the highest-intent signal when multiple are active. Shopping Assistant session outranks cart abandon — purchase blocker is already resolved, context is already in hand.
The Shift

Today vs. container

Today
Cart abandon flow fires when a cart is abandoned — regardless of what else is happening with the shopper.
Campaign blasts everyone in the segment at the scheduled send time — with no awareness of what flows are already running for that shopper.
Flows and campaigns run independently — a shopper can get a cart recovery and a campaign blast in the same day for different products.
Marketer configures a trigger and sequence for each flow type.
Container
Container evaluates all active signals for each shopper and decides which to pursue — or whether to wait.
Campaign always reaches the full eligible segment. Container personalizes each message and suppresses conflicting flows during the campaign window.
Campaign always wins during its window. Flows hold and re-evaluate after. No shopper gets a flow message while a campaign is active.
Marketer states a goal once. AI handles execution per shopper, per signal.
